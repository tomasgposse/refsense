# Finding references

The goal is a small set of excellent references, not a big set of relevant ones. Five pages that are clearly better than average will produce sharper patterns than fifteen ordinary ones.

## Where to look, in order

1. **The category's best-known brands.** Who are the 3–5 names people in this space admire? A specialty coffee roaster → look at the roasters that designers and coffee people talk about; a dev tool → the tools developers praise for their sites. Search: `best <category> websites`, `<category> brand design`, `<leading brand> website`. Their pages are designed by strong teams and tested on real traffic.
2. **Curated galleries.** These exist precisely to collect good work:
   - Landing pages: land-book.com, lapa.ninja, onepagelove.com, saaslandingpage.com, landingfolio.com
   - High craft / award level: godly.website, awwwards.com, siteinspire.com, minimal.gallery, httpster.net
   - App screens: mobbin.com and refero.design (often require login — use them only if you can read them; otherwise skip)
   Search inside them by category (`<category> site:lapa.ninja`, `<category> site:land-book.com`) or browse the category page.
3. **Adjacent categories** when the category itself is thin (see below).

Open each candidate. Gallery thumbnails flatter; the live page is what you're judging.

## Query templates

- `best <category> website design <current year>`
- `<category> landing page inspiration`
- `<category> site:land-book.com` / `site:lapa.ninja` / `site:godly.website`
- `<well-known brand in category> website` (then look at who they're compared with)
- For local businesses: `<category> <big city known for design: Copenhagen, Tokyo, NYC, London, Buenos Aires>` — premium local businesses in design-heavy cities set the bar.

## Quality bar — keep a reference only if

- It has a clear point of view: you could describe its style in three words.
- Typography is deliberate: few families, clear hierarchy, comfortable reading width.
- It works on mobile (check it).
- It's a real, live business page — not a template demo, theme showcase or Dribbble shot.
- It isn't mostly stock photography or generic illustration.
- It doesn't look like generic AI output (see anti-patterns in `pattern-catalog.md`).

Also aim for **variety within the set**: if all eight references come from one gallery, they'll share that gallery's taste rather than the category's logic. Mix sources.

## Matching references to the brand

References should match the brand's **audience and price level**, not only its category. A luxury hotel and a backpacker hostel are both "accommodation" but should learn from very different pages. Use the brief: tone and audience decide which references fit.

## Adjacent categories

When a category has few good sites, borrow from categories that share the same visitor intent:

| Category | Look at |
|---|---|
| Tire shop, mechanic | Premium car workshops, car detailing, bike shops |
| Plumber, electrician, trades | Architecture studios' clarity + modern home services startups |
| Dentist, clinic | Wellness studios, modern health startups, premium skincare |
| Accountant, lawyer | Fintech, consulting firms, private banks |
| Local restaurant, café | Specialty coffee roasters, natural wine bars, bakeries with strong brands |
| Gym, trainer | Boutique fitness studios, athletic apparel brands |
| School, course | Design/coding bootcamps, education startups |
| Event / activation agency | Experiential studios, production houses, creative agencies |
| Motorsport, racing team | Performance car brands, cycling teams, motorsport apparel |
| Real estate | Boutique hotels, architecture studios |
| Personal finance app | Fintech and neobank apps, budgeting apps |

Say which adjacent categories you used and why, so the user understands where the patterns come from.

## When to stop and ask

After a genuine search (several queries, at least two galleries, category leaders checked), if you have fewer than 4 strong references — or the brand is so specific that outside references would mislead — ask the user for a folder:

> I couldn't find enough good references for <category>. If you put screenshots or links of sites you like in a folder (even 3–4) and tell me the path, I'll work from those. Sites from other categories are fine too — what matters is that you like how they feel.

Don't lower the bar to reach a number. Three great references are better than six mediocre ones; say that you're working with three.
